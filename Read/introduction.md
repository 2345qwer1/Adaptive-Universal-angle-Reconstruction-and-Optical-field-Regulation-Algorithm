In this work, we identify an intrinsic and universal physical limitation of VAM: the non-negativity of optical intensity prevents tomographic dose accumulation from realizing the signed contributions required by an unconstrained mathematical reconstruction. This fundamental mismatch is inherent to the physical principle of VAM and causes local dose contrast, and consequently local resolution, to vary with spatial position and feature orientation even in an otherwise ideal optical system. To visualize and theoretically characterize this limitation, we compare a signed mathematical reference solution with its physically realizable non-negative counterpart under otherwise identical conditions, thereby isolating the resolution degradation introduced solely by the physical non-negativity constraint. Radial and angular test structures are then used to map the resulting spatial variation in local target-to-background dose contrast and thresholded printout fidelity. From these measurements, we determine the position- and orientation-dependent limiting resolvable feature size throughout the build volume, rather than representing VAM resolution by a single global value. The analysis is further extended to different optical attenuation conditions and validated through physical printing experiments. These results establish spatially heterogeneous resolution as an intrinsic limitation of VAM and provide a quantitative framework for visualizing, explaining, and determining its local resolution limit.
